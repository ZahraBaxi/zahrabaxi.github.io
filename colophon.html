why a colophon?

A colophon is a document that describes the tools and technologies used in the development of a publication. In the context of a website, it serves a similar purpose, providing transparency about the technical aspects of the site. My introduction to the colophon was through many physical bookbinding publications I've been involved in, one of which is archived in the Bancroft Library at UC Berkeley. I've been creating colophons for specific projects for a while, but lately I've been seeing more and more designers and folks from other fields include colophons for their portfolio or website as a whole, as a way to showcase their process and tools.

philosophy

This site started in high school, when I was just learning the basics of web design to build a place for my art portfolio. It's since expanded into what it is now, but it's still an ongoing process and, honestly, an ongoing learning process too. Every time I pick up a new skill or get interested in something new, I come back and rework a piece of it. The purpose of this colophon is to document the tools, technologies, and design choices of the current version of the site.

design

Ideations and prototype designs are made in Figma ↗, then coded from scratch by hand. I keep the palette simple and type-driven: a warm cream background, near-black text, and a single accent blue used sparingly for links and highlights.

development

This site is hand-coded in plain HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript, no frameworks, using Visual Studio Code ↗. While that adds a bit of complexity, it also keeps things simple by not relying on external libraries. Pages share a header and footer through a small partials.js script, mostly so I only have to update the copyright year in one place. Version-controlled with Git, and files pushed via GitHub Desktop ↗. Served for free via GitHub Pages ↗, with the source living on a public repo since 2019 (and seeing many, many evolutions). The domain is registered through Namecheap ↗. Contact and bug report forms run on Back4App ↗, a cloud backend built on Parse. I use Google Search Console ↗ to make sure pages stay properly indexed.

typography

  • PP Neue Montreal ↗ by Pangram Pangram Foundry, used for headings and body copy. Free for personal use.
  • Font Awesome ↗, used for icons throughout the site.

inspiration

This latest version of the site, particularly the footer, was inspired by Karolina Szczur's website ↗.

land acknowledgement

I live and work on the traditional homelands of two Indigenous nations: the Nisenan people, whose ancestral territory includes present-day Sacramento, and the Patwin people, whose territory includes present-day Davis and the Yolo Basin, where much of my thesis research took place. Both peoples have stewarded this land since time immemorial, and both continue to do so today despite the profound disruption of colonization. I pay respect to their Elders past and present, and to all Indigenous people connected to this place.
