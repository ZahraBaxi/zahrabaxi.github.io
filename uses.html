uses

the hardware, software, apps, and physical stuff i actually use to make things. inspired by uses.tech ↗.

hardware

Computer — MacBook. Files live locally, pushed to GitHub via GitHub Desktop.

Tablet — iPad with Apple Pencil — mainly for sketching, annotation, and GoodNotes.

Printing — Canon PIXMA MegaTank for home printing. Does a great job on zines and letter-size prints without destroying an ink budget.

Cutting — Cricut Maker 4 for precision cutting, vinyl, and anything the scissors can't handle cleanly.

cameras

Photography is something I come back to in a lot of different forms depending on what I'm making and what I want the image to feel like.

Canon EOS Elan (35mm film) — My main film body. I shoot with lenses from Canon's EF Ultrasonic line, including a telephoto. There's something about committing to a frame on film that changes how I look at a scene.

Fujifilm X-T3 — My mirrorless digital. Fast, reliable, gorgeous color science. This is what I reach for when I need to document work or shoot anything that needs to be edited quickly.

Lumix mirrorless — Used this for the On San Pablo project — good for long documentary days on foot.

Disposable film cameras — For when I want something lo-fi and unprecious. Great for events and moments where I don't want to think too hard.

Polaroid (retro) & Instax Mini 11 — For instant prints. The Instax lives in my bag more often than not.

I shoot on FujiFilm 400, Kodak Gold 400, Kodak 200, and Portra 400 & 800 depending on the light.

audio

I live pretty firmly in the Apple audio universe for everyday listening — AirPods Max with Apple Music is genuinely one of the best listening experiences I've had. The noise cancellation is unreal for working.

But I also have a Sony Sports Walkman and Sony Walkman headphones for radio and cassettes, and a CD Walkman too. There's something really nice about listening to a physical medium without any shuffle or algorithm involved.

software & coding

VS Code — My main editor. Where most of this site was written.

Atom — Still use it occasionally depending on the project.

RStudio — For data analysis and visualization. R is surprisingly enjoyable once you get past the syntax.

Pure Data (Pd) — For live coding music and audio experiments. Running Purr Data on Mac. Still learning but it's a whole world.

Obsidian — For notes, research, and keeping my thesis reading somewhat organized. Local-first, which I appreciate.

Figma — For all design work — wireframes, layouts, zine mockups, anything visual before it becomes code or print.

Adobe Creative Suite — Illustrator and Photoshop mainly. Fresco for illustration on the iPad.

ArcGIS & QGIS — For spatial and mapping work. QGIS when I don't want to deal with a license.

See the colophon for the full breakdown of how this site specifically is built.

apps

GoodNotes — One-time purchase on iPad. Worth every penny. I use it for annotating PDFs, sketchnoting in meetings, and keeping thesis notes alongside Obsidian.

Apple Music — Pairs perfectly with the AirPods Max. The lossless audio quality matters more than I expected.

physical making

Zines — Home printer, standard letter-size paper, folded by hand. That's the whole setup. The constraint is kind of the point.

Embroidery — Done entirely by hand with an embroidery hoop. No machine — just needle, thread, and patience. I find it meditative in a way that most screen work isn't.

Knitting & sewing/mending — Mostly by hand or with whatever machine is available. I tend to mend and repair more than I buy new.

Sketchbooks — Pen over pencil most of the time. I go through sketchbooks faster than anything else.
