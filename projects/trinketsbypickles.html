overview

Trinkets by Pickles was my small handmade business. I designed and made small-batch goods, including stickers, earrings and charms, buttons and pins, prints, tote bags, and rugs, and sold them in person at local markets in Davis, CA.

The work blended nature and video game inspirations. Everything was made by hand in small batches, so there was no mass production and very little waste.

I ran every part of it: designing the products, making them, setting prices, building the website, and talking with customers at the market. It was a hands-on way to practice product design, because each item had to be useful or delightful enough that someone chose to buy it.

the product line

The shop was organized by product type: stickers, earrings and charms, buttons and pins, prints, tote bags, and rugs. Each type had its own materials, making process, and use, so the line ranged from small pieces to wear or collect to everyday items like tote bags.

selling in person

I sold only in person. My main spot was the Davis Street Market on Saturdays from 10a to 3p when the weather was good. I stayed home if it was raining, too windy, or over 105°F. Setup started at 8:30, and I often packed up early depending on sales.

Selling face to face showed me what people picked up, what they asked about, and what they walked past. That feedback shaped what I made next.

I took cash, Venmo, and Tap to Pay, in that order of preference. Venmo and Tap to Pay came with small service fees that were passed on to the customer, so cash was the cheapest way to pay. I accepted bills of $20 and smaller and gave change.

the website

I built the site from scratch with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, and hosted it on GitHub. It had three parts: home, find me, and about.

The home page worked as a catalog. Visitors could filter products by type, from stickers to rugs.

The find me page listed where I would be next and included a live market status board with a location, a status, and a short message. Weather could change my plans, so regulars could check the board before they walked over. I updated it before, during, and after each market.

reflection

Running a small business meant every decision was mine and every result was visible. Small details mattered at a table: how a product was displayed, how clear a price was, how fast checkout felt.

It also taught me to design within real limits, like handmade batch sizes, material costs, and weather.
